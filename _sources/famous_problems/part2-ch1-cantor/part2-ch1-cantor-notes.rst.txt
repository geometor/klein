Part II, Chapter I — Critical & Symbolic Notes
==============================================

.. rubric:: Cantor's 1874 vs. 1891 Proofs, Golden Section at Height $N = 4$, and Foundational Impact

Historical & Sister-Corpus Notes
--------------------------------

* **Klein's Synthesis of Cantor 1874 and Halle 1891:** In :person:`Cantor's <cantor>` original 1874 paper (:cite:`*Crelle's Journal*, Vol. 77, pp. 258–262 <cantor:1874>`), the uncountability of every interval $[\alpha, \beta]$ was proved by nested intervals $[a_\nu, b_\nu]$ skipping sequence elements, whereas his 1891 Halle note introduced the pure diagonal argument on binary sequences without mentioning algebraic numbers. Klein's 1895 Göttingen lecture is the historic text that **fused Cantor's 1874 algebraic-height enumeration with the 1891 decimal diagonal construction** (explicitly excluding trailing $9$'s to ensure decimal uniqueness)—creating the canonical exposition taught in mathematics ever since!
* **Golden Ratio and Root-Two at Height $N = 4$ (`geometor/divine` & `geometor/fundamentals`):** Notice that the very first irrational numbers to appear in Cantor's height hierarchy occur at $N = 4$ ($n = 2$, $|a_0| + |a_1| + |a_2| = 3$), consisting of **precisely two families**: the diagonal of the unit square $\pm\sqrt{2}, \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ ($\omega^2 - 2 = 0$, $2\omega^2 - 1 = 0$) and the **golden section** $\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ with its conjugates $\frac{\pm 1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$ ($\omega^2 \mp \omega - 1 = 0$)!
* **Continuum & Foundations (`geometor/hilbert`, `geometor/bertrand-russell`, `geometor/hoenen`):** Klein's contrast between countable algebraic point-fields (such as Hilbert's constructible pythagorean/quadratic fields $\Omega$ in :cite:`*Grundlagen der Geometrie*, 1899 <hilbert:1899>`) and the full Dedekind–Cantor continuum informs both :person:`Bertrand Russell's <russell>` analysis of continuity (:cite:`1897 <russell:1897>`) and :person:`P. H. J. Hoenen's <hoenen>` epistemological critique of geometric intelligibility vs. arithmetic set constructions (:cite:`1954 <hoenen:1954>`).
