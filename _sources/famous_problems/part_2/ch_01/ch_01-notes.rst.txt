Part II, Chapter I — Critical & Symbolic Notes
==============================================

.. rubric:: Cantor's 1874 vs. 1891 Proofs, Golden Section at Height :math:`N = 4`, and Foundational Impact

Historical & Sister-Corpus Notes
--------------------------------

* **Klein's Synthesis of Cantor 1874 and Halle 1891:** In :person:`Cantor's <cantor>` original 1874 paper (:cite:`*Crelle's Journal*, Vol. 77, pp. 258–262 <cantor:1874>`), the uncountability of every interval :math:`[\alpha, \beta]` was proved by nested intervals :math:`[a_\nu, b_\nu]` skipping sequence elements, whereas his 1891 Halle note introduced the pure diagonal argument on binary sequences without mentioning algebraic numbers. Klein's 1895 Göttingen lecture is the historic text that **fused Cantor's 1874 algebraic-height enumeration with the 1891 decimal diagonal construction** (explicitly excluding trailing :math:`9`'s to ensure decimal uniqueness)—creating the canonical exposition taught in mathematics ever since!
* **Golden Ratio and Root-Two at Height** :math:`N = 4` (**`geometor/divine` & `geometor/fundamentals`**): Notice that the very first irrational numbers to appear in Cantor's height hierarchy occur at :math:`N = 4` (:math:`n = 2`, :math:`|a_0| + |a_1| + |a_2| = 3`), consisting of **precisely two families**: the diagonal of the unit square :math:`\pm\sqrt{2}, \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}` (:math:`\omega^2 - 2 = 0`, :math:`2\omega^2 - 1 = 0`) and the **golden section** :math:`\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}` with its conjugates :math:`\frac{\pm 1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}` (:math:`\omega^2 \mp \omega - 1 = 0`)!
* **Continuum & Foundations (`geometor/hilbert`, `geometor/bertrand-russell`, `geometor/hoenen`):** Klein's contrast between countable algebraic point-fields (such as Hilbert's constructible pythagorean/quadratic fields :math:`\Omega` in :cite:`*Grundlagen der Geometrie*, 1899 <hilbert:1899>`) and the full Dedekind–Cantor continuum informs both :person:`Bertrand Russell's <russell>` analysis of continuity (:cite:`1897 <russell:1897>`) and :person:`P. H. J. Hoenen's <hoenen>` epistemological critique of geometric intelligibility vs. arithmetic set constructions (:cite:`1954 <hoenen:1954>`).
