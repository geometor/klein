Notes I–VII: Historical & Foundational Appendices — Summary
===========================================================

.. rubric:: Analytical Synopsis of Klein's Seven Philosophical, Axiomatic, and Invariant Appendices

* **Note I — Synthesis vs. Analysis:** Reconciles the nineteenth-century rivalry between synthetic geometers (:person:`Poncelet <poncelet>`, :person:`Steiner <steiner>`, :person:`von Staudt <staudt>`) and algebraic analysts (:person:`Plücker <pluecker>`, :person:`Hesse <hesse>`, :person:`Clebsch <clebsch>`) under the unified banner of *projective geometry*: algebraic formalism leaps ahead of thought in discovery, yet no investigation is complete until made conceptually and intuitively transparent.
* **Note II — Overcoming Disciplinary Fragmentation:** Calls for mutual fertilization between mathematical physics, projective geometry, and differential surface curvature.
* **Note III — Autonomous Value of Spatial Intuition (*Anschauung*):** Clarifies that while *abstract* group theory treats spatial figures merely as pedagogical illustrations, there exists an autonomous *anschauliche Geometrie* for which physical or vividly imagined spatial models are the primary object of knowledge.
* **Note IV — $n$-Dimensional Manifolds Without Mysticism:** Sharply separates the mathematical theory of $n$-dimensional :term:`manifolds <manifold>` (:person:`Grassmann <grassmann>` 1844, :person:`Riemann <riemann>` 1854) and parameter spaces (:person:`Plücker <pluecker>`) from popular metaphysical or spiritualist claims about a fourth physical dimension.
* **Note V — Non-Euclidean Geometry & Axiom Independence:** Explains why the main text avoided the polemical label *non-Euclidean geometry*, then highlights its two permanent mathematical achievements:
  1. Proving **independence of the Parallel Postulate**—and proposing systematic **mutual independence proofs for every axiom** across mathematics (directly anticipating :person:`David Hilbert's <hilbert>` :cite:`*Grundlagen der Geometrie*, 1899 <hilbert:1899>`)!
  2. Establishing **constant-curvature Cayley–Klein projective measurement**, containing Euclidean geometry as a limiting degenerate case while leaving empirical/epistemological debates over physical space to philosophy (qualified in 1893 fn. 41: philosophers *cannot* responsibly discuss space without mastering the mathematical models!).
* **Note VI — Metric Geometry on Quadrics and Line Complexes:** Shows that the ambient Cayley–Klein cross-ratio vanishes identically on the boundary quadric itself; inducing an intrinsic constant- or zero-curvature metric on the quadric (or on Plücker's line-space) requires fixing an external point/polar plane (or a general vs. special linear line complex).
* **Note VII — Spherical Symmetry of Binary Cubics & Quartics:** Visualizes binary invariants on the Riemann sphere: a cubic $f$, its Hessian $\Delta$ (north/south poles), and cubic covariant $Q$ ($60^\circ$-rotated equator); and a quartic pencil $i H + \lambda j f$ as symmetric tetrahedra $(x,y,z), (x,-y,-z), (-x,y,-z), (-x,-y,z)$ whose opposite edges are bisected by the octahedral axes of the sextic covariant $T$—geometrically explaining Lagrange's/Galois's **cubic resolvent of the quartic**!
