§§ 3–5: Projective Geometry & Space-Elements — Critical & Symbolic Notes¶

Algebraic Group Isomorphisms, the Klein Quadric in $\mathbb{P}^5$, and 1893 Revisions

Classical Lie-Group Isomorphisms Discovered in §§ 4–5¶

Klein’s geometric transference arguments in §§ 4–5 establish several of the celebrated low-dimensional exceptional isomorphisms of classical Lie algebras and algebraic groups without a single matrix computation:

  1. $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb{C}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{PO}_3(\mathbb{C})$ (§§ 4–5): Via the Veronese conic embedding $[u : v] \mapsto [u^2 : uv : v^2]$, binary projective transformations act as projective orthogonal transformations preserving the conic $x z - y^2 = 0$, identifying binary invariant theory with planar non-Euclidean Cayley–Klein geometry.

  2. $\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbb{C}) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2 \;\cong\; \operatorname{PO}_6(\mathbb{C})$ (§ 5 — The Plücker–Klein Quadric): Representing a line joining $X = (x_0 : x_1 : x_2 : x_3)$ and $Y = (y_0 : y_1 : y_2 : y_3)$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$ by its six $2 \times 2$ Plücker determinants $p_{ij} = x_i y_j - x_j y_i$ embeds the Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(2, 4)$ as the smooth $4$-dimensional quadric

    \[\Omega_{\text{Plücker}}(p) \;=\; p_{01} p_{23} + p_{02} p_{31} + p_{03} p_{12} \;=\; 0 \quad \subset \mathbb{P}^5,\]

    where spatial collineations and dualities correspond to the two connected components of orthogonal transformations preserving $\Omega_{\text{Plücker}} = 0$.

Two Important 1893 Revisions by Klein¶

  • Footnote 14 (1872 *Chasles* $\to$ 1893 *[the French school]*): In the 1872 Erlangen pamphlet (fn. 13), Klein attributed the interpretation of metric properties via the imaginary spherical circle at infinity solely to Michel Chasles. Recognizing the earlier foundational priority of Jean-Victor Poncelet (1822) and Edmond Laguerre (1853, angle via logarithm of cross-ratio with $I, J$), Klein broadened the 1893 wording to [the French school].

  • Footnote 23 (1893 Addition on Normal Subgroups and Faithful Orbits): In § 5, Klein defines a Körper (“body,” modern homogeneous space $G / H$) as the orbit of a geometric element under $G$. In 1893, Klein added Footnote 23 pointing out that if the stabilizer $H = \operatorname{Stab}_G(x_0)$ contains a nontrivial self-conjugate (normal) subgroup $N \triangleleft G$, then $N$ acts trivially on every element $g \cdot x_0$ of the orbit ($N \subset g H g^{-1}$), so the orbit represents only the quotient group $G / N$ rather than $G$ faithfully. For instance, points at infinity fail to represent translations inside the affine or Euclidean group because translations act trivially on the plane at infinity!

  • Pappus & Projective Cross-Ratio (`geometor/pappus`): See Pappus, Collection Book VII for the ancient root of projective invariance and von Staudt's Geometrie der Lage discussed in § 3.
