§§ 1–2: The Principal Group and Transformation Hierarchy — Summary¶

Analytical Structure of Klein’s Axiomatic Definition of a Geometry

§ 1. Groups of Transformations, the Principal Group, and the Erlangen Problem¶

  1. Axiomatic Group Closure (and Inverses): Klein defines a transformation group $G$ on space (or on an $n$-dimensional manifold $M$) as a family of transformations closed under composition—adding in his famous 1893 bracketed footnote that for infinite families one must explicitly stipulate closure under inverses ($g \in G \implies g^{-1} \in G$). While dualities (correlations) alone do not form a group because the product of two dualities is a collineation, the union of collineations and dualities forms an index-$2$ group extension.

  2. The Principal Group (*Hauptgruppe*): Elementary Euclidean properties depend neither on absolute spatial position (translations and rotations), nor on absolute scale (homotheties), nor on chiral orientation (mirror reflections). Their composite group is the $7$-parameter spatial (or $4$-parameter planar) Principal Group of similarities. Elementary geometry is defined by invariance under the Principal Group.

  3. The Erlangen Problem: Abstracting from physical space to any manifold $M$ equipped with an arbitrarily chosen transformation group $G$, Klein states the universal program of modern geometry:

    \[\text{Geometry}(M, G) \;\equiv\; \text{Invariant Theory of } G \text{ acting on } M.\]

§ 2. Stabilizer Subgroups and Distinguished Fundamental Loci¶

Klein establishes the Galois-style duality between restricting the group and adjoining a fixed geometric locus:

  • Stabilizer Principle (Descending to a Subgroup $H \subset G$): Studying configurations on $M$ relative to a fixed figure $\Omega$ under a larger group $G$ is mathematically identical to studying configurations by themselves under the stabilizer subgroup

    \[\operatorname{Stab}_G(\Omega) \;=\; \{\, g \in G \mid g(\Omega) = \Omega \,\}.\]

    For example, spherical trigonometry is the invariant theory of the $3$-parameter rotation subgroup fixing a center $O$ inside the Principal Group.

  • Ascent to Supergroups ($G_{\text{sim}} \subset G$): Replacing the Principal Group $G_{\text{sim}}$ by a larger group $G$ (such as affine, projective, or inversion transformations) retains only a subset of elementary invariants as intrinsic properties; all remaining elementary metric properties reappear as joint invariants of the figure together with a distinguished locus $\Omega$ whose stabilizer in $G$ is $G_{\text{sim}}$.
