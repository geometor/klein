Notes I–VII: Non-Euclidean Metrics & Resolvent Symmetries — Critical & Symbolic Notes¶

Exact Symbolic Cayley–Klein Additivity (geometor.klein.erlangen) and Sister-Corpus Concordance

Cayley–Klein Projective Metric Additivity (Note V & Note VI)¶

In geometor.klein.erlangen.verify_cayley_klein_additivity(), we verify exact projective metric additivity in SymPy over $\mathbb{Q}$ on the diameter $(-1, 0)$ to $(1, 0)$ of the real unit-circle Absolute $x^2 + y^2 - 1 = 0$:

  • Taking collinear interior points $A\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}, 0\right)$, $B\!\left(\frac{1}{5}, 0\right)$, $C\!\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ between ideal points $I_1(-1, 0)$ and $I_2(1, 0)$ inside geometor.model.Model:

    \[(A, B; I_1, I_2) \;=\; \frac{9}{2}, \qquad (B, C; I_1, I_2) \;=\; \frac{7}{2}, \qquad (A, C; I_1, I_2) \;=\; \frac{63}{4} \;=\; \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{2}.\]

  • Because the cross-ratio telescopes multiplicatively along every chord ($(A, B; I_1, I_2) \cdot (B, C; I_1, I_2) - (A, C; I_1, I_2) = 0$ identically), Klein’s logarithmic metric satisfies exact additivity:

    \[d(A, B) + d(B, C) \;=\; c \ln\!\left(\tfrac{9}{2}\right) + c \ln\!\left(\tfrac{7}{2}\right) \;=\; c \ln\!\left(\tfrac{63}{4}\right) \;=\; d(A, C).\]

Note V’s Direct Prophecy of Hilbert’s Grundlagen (1899)¶

Look closely at paragraph 3 of Note V:

“Similar investigations could and should be performed with regard to every axiom (and not alone in geometry); an insight would thus be obtained into the mutual relation of the axioms.”

Twenty-seven years later, Klein’s Göttingen colleague David Hilbert carried out this exact program in Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899) (geometor/hilbert), constructing algebraic coordinate fields to prove the mutual independence of Incidence, Order, Congruence, Continuity/Archimedes, and Parallels! Meanwhile, Klein’s 1893 Footnote 41 directly sets the stage for Bertrand Russell's 1897 Essay on the Foundations of Geometry (geometor/bertrand-russell) and P. H. J. Hoenen's 1954 De Noetica Geometriae (geometor/hoenen).

Note VII & Quartic Resolvent Constructibility¶

Klein’s tetrahedral configuration $(x,y,z), (x,-y,-z), (-x,y,-z), (-x,-y,z)$ on the Riemann sphere in Note VII gives the geometric heart of geometor.klein.constructibility.cubic_resolvent: the Klein four-group $V_4 = \{1, R_x, R_y, R_z\} \triangleleft S_4$ of $180^\circ$ rotations around the three coordinate axes $OX, OY, OZ$ preserves the four vertices of every tetrahedron in the syzygetic pencil $i H + \lambda j f$, while the quotient group $S_4 / V_4 \cong S_3$ permutes the three coordinate axes (the six octahedral vertices of $T$). Consequently, solving a quartic equation reduces to solving the degree-$3$ resolvent for the three pairs of opposite edge midpoints—which is constructible by straightedge and compasses if and only if that cubic resolvent splits over $\mathbb{Q}$ (as verified in geometor.klein.constructibility for $x^4 - 2$ vs. $x^4 - x - 1$)!
