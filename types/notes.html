Part II, Ch. I: Cantor’s Transcendental Numbers — Critical & Symbolic Notes

Klein’s Synthesis of Cantor 1874 and Halle 1891: In Cantor's original 1874 paper (Crelle's Journal, Vol. 77, pp. 258–262), the uncountability of every interval \([\alpha, \beta]\) was proved by nested intervals \([a_\nu, b_\nu]\) skipping sequence elements, whereas his 1891 Halle note introduced the pure diagonal argument on binary sequences without mentioning algebraic numbers. Klein’s 1895 Göttingen lecture is the historic text that fused Cantor’s 1874 algebraic-height enumeration with the 1891 decimal diagonal construction (explicitly excluding trailing \(9\)’s to ensure decimal uniqueness)—creating the canonical exposition taught in mathematics ever since!

Part II, Ch. III: Transcendence of e — Critical & Symbolic Notes

Why Gordan’s Symbol \(h^r = r!\) Replaces Hilbert’s Integral: In Hilbert's 1893 note (Göttinger Nachrichten), the multiplier \(M\) was defined as the improper integral \(I = \int_0^\infty \varphi(z) e^{-z}\,dz\), and multiplying by \(e^k\) split \(\int_0^\infty \varphi(z) e^{k-z}\,dz\) at \(z = k\) into \(\int_k^\infty = \int_0^\infty \varphi(h + k) e^{-h}\,dh\) (an integer divisible by \(p\)) plus the finite segment \(e^k \int_0^k \varphi(z) e^{-z}\,dz\) (which shrinks to \(0\) as \(p \to \infty\)). Gordan (1893) observed that the linear functional \(\mathcal{L}(z^r) = \int_0^\infty z^r e^{-z}\,dz = r!\) acts purely algebraically on polynomials, and that the series identity \(e^k \cdot r! = (h + k)^r + k^r \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{k^m}{(r+1)\cdots(r+m)}\) reproduces the exact same integer-plus-tail decomposition without a single integral sign!
