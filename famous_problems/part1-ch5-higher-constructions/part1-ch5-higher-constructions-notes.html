Part I, Chapter V — Critical & Symbolic Notes¶

Bibliographic Variants (Sundara Row 1893), Circular Points at Infinity, and Origami Cubics

Textual & Mathematical Commentary¶

  • Sundara Row Citation (1895 DE vs. 1897 EN): In 1895 DE (S. 33), Klein cited T. Sundara Row's book from memory as „On paper folding“ (London, Macmillan, 1893). In preparing the 1897 English translation (p. 42), Beman and Smith procured a physical copy directly from India and restored the exact title and imprint: Geometrical Exercises in Paper Folding (Madras: Addison & Co., 1893)—subsequently editing the famous Open Court American edition of 1901! (Modern axiomatization of paper folding via the Huzita–Justin axioms proves that single-fold common tangents to two parabolas solve arbitrary cubic equations, including $\sqrt[3]{2}$ and angle trisection.)

  • Projective Role of the Circular Points at Infinity (`geometor/pappus` & Erlangen Program): Notice how effortlessly Klein uses projective algebraic geometry in §§ 3–4: homogenizing $(x^2 + y^2)x - y^2 z = 0$ and $(x^2 + y^2)(x - az)^2 - b^2 x^2 z^2 = 0$ at $z = 0$ gives $(x^2 + y^2)x = 0$ and $(x^2 + y^2)x^2 = 0$, showing that both the cissoid and the conchoid pass through Poncelet's imaginary circular points $(1 : \pm i : 0)$—the very Absolute of Euclidean metric geometry highlighted in Erlangen Program Note V!

  • Ancient Classification of Problems (`geometor/pappus`): Klein’s separation of straightedge-and-compasses constructions (Chapters I–IV), conic-section solid constructions (§ 2), and higher-curve linear constructions (§§ 3–5) mirrors Pappus's famous tripartite classification (plane, solid, and linear problems) in Collection, Book III & Book IV, now grounded in exact Galois degree and Bézout intersection multiplicity.
