Part II, Chapter II — Analytical Summary¶

Five Epochs of Circle Measurement from Ahmes to Gordan (§§ 1–5)

Chronological Architecture (Following Rudio 1892)¶

Drawing on Ferdinand Rudio's historical anthology (1892), Klein organizes $4000$ years of circle-squaring research into five methodological epochs:

Five Historical Stages in the Problem of $\pi$¶

Stage

Key Figures & Dates

Mathematical Milestone

§ 1. Empirical

Ahmes (c. 2000 B.C.); 1 Kings 7:23

Mensuration rules: Egyptian $\pi \approx (16/9)^2 \approx 3.1605$; Biblical $\pi \approx 3$.

§ 2. Classical Greek

Hippias, Dinostratus, Archimedes, Huygens (1654)

Rigorous polygonal exhaustion ($3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}$) and kinematic quadratrix $x = \frac{y}{\tan(\pi y/2)} \to \frac{2}{\pi}$.

§ 3. Analytic Series

Napier (1614), Leibniz, Euler (1670–1770)

Infinite series ($\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \dots$) and Euler’s bridge $e^{ix} = \cos x + i\sin x \implies 1 + e^{i\pi} = 0$.

§ 4. Irrationality

Lambert (1770), Legendre (1794)

Continued-fraction proofs that $\pi \notin \mathbb{Q}$ and $\pi^2 \notin \mathbb{Q}$ (leaving open whether $\pi \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$).

§ 5. Transcendence

Hermite (1873), Lindemann (1882), Weierstrass (1885), Hilbert, Hurwitz, Gordan (1893)

Proof that $e \notin \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\pi \notin \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, reduced in Spring 1893 to purely elementary polynomial divisibility modulo $p$.
