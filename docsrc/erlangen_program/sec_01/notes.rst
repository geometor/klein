§ 1: Principal Group & The Group Concept — Critical & Symbolic Notes
====================================================================

.. rubric:: Invariant Characterization of Geometry & The Axiom of Group Inverses

The Erlangen Invariant Definition of Geometry
---------------------------------------------

In § 1, Felix Klein formulates his celebrated program:

   *“Given a manifoldness and a group of transformations of the same; to investigate the configurations belonging to the manifoldness with regard to such properties as are not altered by the transformations of the group.”*

* **The Principal Group:** The totality of Euclidean motions, reflections, and isotropic dilations (similarities) forming the 7-parameter group :math:`\operatorname{Sim}(\mathbb{R}^3) = (\mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes \operatorname{O}_3) \times \mathbb{R}^+`.
* **Invariant Characterization:** Geometric properties are not merely unchanged by the principal group; they are *defined* by their invariance under it.

Philological Note on Footnote 8 (Group Inverses, 1893)
------------------------------------------------------

In 1872, following the finite permutation terminology of :person:`Galois <galois>`, Serret, and Camille Jordan, Klein defined a group solely by closure under composition (:math:`g_1, g_2 \in G \implies g_1 g_2 \in G`). For a *finite* set of bijections, closure automatically implies :math:`g^{-1} = g^{k-1} \in G`; for *infinite* continuous families (such as contractions :math:`x \mapsto \lambda x` with :math:`0 < \lambda < 1`), closure alone defines only a semigroup. Klein's 1893 bracketed addition in Footnote 8 explicitly supplies the inverse axiom required by :person:`Sophus Lie's <lie>` *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen*.
