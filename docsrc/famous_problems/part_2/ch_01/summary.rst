Part II, Chapter I — Analytical Summary
=======================================

.. rubric:: Countability of :math:`\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{R}` vs. Uncountability of :math:`\mathbb{R}` (§§ 1–3)

Logical Structure of Part II's Opening Argument
-----------------------------------------------

Before proving that the specific constants :math:`e` and :math:`\pi` are transcendental (Chapters III–IV), Klein opens Part II with :person:`Georg Cantor's <cantor>` set-theoretic demonstration (:cite:`1874 <cantor:1874>`, Halle 1891) that :term:`transcendental numbers <transcendental-number>` not only exist, but overwhelm the algebraic numbers in every sub-interval of the line:

1. **Dense** :math:`\neq` **Continuous (§ 1**): Rational numbers are dense (*überall dicht*) on :math:`\mathbb{R}`, and real algebraic numbers :math:`\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{R}` (roots of :math:`a_0 \omega^n + \dots + a_n = 0`, :math:`a_i \in \mathbb{Z}`) are denser still—yet neither exhausts the continuum.
2. **Enumeration by Algebraic Height (§ 2):** Assigning each irreducible coprime integer polynomial (:math:`a_0 > 0`) its :term:`height <algebraic-height>` :math:`N = n - 1 + \sum_{i=0}^n |a_i|` partitions :math:`\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{R}` into finite layers (:math:`\varphi(1) = 1`, :math:`\varphi(2) = 2`, :math:`\varphi(3) = 4`, :math:`\varphi(4) = 12, \dots`), placing all real algebraic numbers in bijection (*gleiche Mächtigkeit*) with :math:`\mathbb{Z}^+`.
3. **Diagonal Avoidance (§ 3):** Fixing any finite decimal prefix (hence any sub-interval) and choosing each subsequent digit :math:`\neq 9` and :math:`\neq` the diagonal digit of :math:`\omega_k` produces :math:`8^{\aleph_0}` non-algebraic reals in that interval alone.
