Part II, Chapters IV–V — Critical & Symbolic Notes
==================================================

.. rubric:: Structural Collation (*Anhang* vs. *Chapter V*), Non-Zero Leading Terms in Galois Products, and Non-Holonomic Kinematics

Textual & Mathematical Notes
----------------------------

* **Structural Variant (*Anhang* in 1895 DE vs. *Chapter V* in 1897 EN):** In the 1895 Teubner edition (:cite:`S. 64–66 <klein:1895>`), the three concluding sections on :person:`Abdank-Abakanowicz's <abakanowicz>` :term:`integraph <integraph>` form an unnumbered appendix (*Anhang: Der Integraph und die geometrische Construction von* :math:`\pi`), reflecting Klein's live demonstration of Göttingen's Coradi integraph from the Mathematical Collection at the close of his Easter 1895 teachers' course. In the 1897 Ginn translation (:cite:`pp. 78–80 <klein:1897>`), :person:`Beman <beman>` and :person:`Smith <smith>` numbered this section as **Part II, Chapter V**. Grouping Ch. IV and Ch. V/*Anhang* into Unit 10 preserves 100% structural alignment across both editions.
* **Ensuring a Non-Vanishing Term When Expanding the Galois Product (§ 4):** In § 4, when multiplying :math:`\prod_\alpha \bigl(C'_0 + C'_1 e^{k_\alpha} + C'_2 e^{l_\beta} + \dots\bigr)`, distinct exponent sums :math:`k_{\alpha_1} + l_{\beta_1}` and :math:`k_{\alpha_2} + l_{\beta_2}` could occasionally coincide and cancel. As :person:`Weierstrass <weierstrass>` (1885) and :person:`Gordan <gordan>` (:cite:`1893 <gordan:1893>`) noted, ordering complex numbers lexicographically (first by real part :math:`\operatorname{Re}(z)`, then by imaginary part :math:`\operatorname{Im}(z)`) guarantees a *unique strictly maximal exponent* :math:`\alpha_{\max}` in each factor whose sum :math:`\sum \alpha_{\max}` occurs **once and only once** in the expanded product with non-zero coefficient! Dividing or shifting so :math:`0` is among the surviving exponents then ensures :math:`C_0 \neq 0`.
* **Why the Integraph Succeeds Where Algebraic Linkages Fail:** By Kempe's Universality Theorem (1876), any planar pin-jointed bar linkage (holonomic algebraic constraint) can trace only *algebraic* curves, and therefore by Ch. V § 1 can **never** construct :math:`\pi`. :person:`Abdank-Abakanowicz's <abakanowicz>` integraph (:cite:`1889 <abakanowicz:1889>`) transcends algebraic linkages precisely because its weighted knife-edge roller imposes a **non-holonomic differential constraint** :math:`dY - y\,dX = 0`, physically integrating areas into ordinates!
* **Bridge to Book 2 (*The Erlangen Program*) and ``geometor/wildberger``:** Klein closes *Famous Problems* by showing that transcendental functions (:math:`e^x, \arcsin x`) completely avoid the algebraic lattice :math:`\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^2` save at :math:`(0, 1)` and :math:`(0, 0)`. In :person:`Norman Wildberger's <wildberger>` *Divine Proportions* (:cite:`2005 <wildberger:2005>`, `geometor/wildberger`), replacing transcendental angle measure :math:`\theta = \arcsin s` and distance :math:`\sqrt{Q}` by purely algebraic **quadrance** :math:`Q` and **spread** :math:`s` keeps trigonometry entirely inside the exact algebraic domain :math:`\mathbb{Q}` and its radical extensions—the very invariant arena organized globally by transformation groups in Book 2 (:ref:`The Erlangen Program <ep-index>`).
