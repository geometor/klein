Part II, Chapter II — Analytical Summary
========================================

.. rubric:: Five Epochs of Circle Measurement from Ahmes to Gordan (§§ 1–5)

Chronological Architecture (Following Rudio 1892)
-------------------------------------------------

Drawing on :person:`Ferdinand Rudio's <rudio>` historical anthology (:cite:`1892 <rudio:1892>`), Klein organizes $4000$ years of circle-squaring research into five methodological epochs:

.. list-table:: Five Historical Stages in the Problem of $\pi$
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 28 54

   * - Stage
     - Key Figures & Dates
     - Mathematical Milestone
   * - **§ 1. Empirical**
     - Ahmes (c. 2000 B.C.); 1 Kings 7:23
     - Mensuration rules: Egyptian $\pi \approx (16/9)^2 \approx 3.1605$; Biblical $\pi \approx 3$.
   * - **§ 2. Classical Greek**
     - :person:`Hippias <hippias>`, Dinostratus, :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, :person:`Huygens <huygens>` (1654)
     - Rigorous polygonal exhaustion ($3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}$) and kinematic :term:`quadratrix <quadratrix>` $x = \frac{y}{\tan(\pi y/2)} \to \frac{2}{\pi}$.
   * - **§ 3. Analytic Series**
     - Napier (1614), :person:`Leibniz <leibniz>`, :person:`Euler <euler>` (1670–1770)
     - Infinite series ($\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \dots$) and Euler's bridge $e^{ix} = \cos x + i\sin x \implies 1 + e^{i\pi} = 0$.
   * - **§ 4. Irrationality**
     - :person:`Lambert <lambert>` (1770), :person:`Legendre <legendre>` (1794)
     - Continued-fraction proofs that $\pi \notin \mathbb{Q}$ and $\pi^2 \notin \mathbb{Q}$ (leaving open whether $\pi \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$).
   * - **§ 5. Transcendence**
     - :person:`Hermite <hermite>` (1873), :person:`Lindemann <lindemann>` (1882), :person:`Weierstrass <weierstrass>` (1885), :person:`Hilbert <hilbert>`, :person:`Hurwitz <hurwitz>`, :person:`Gordan <gordan>` (1893)
     - Proof that $e \notin \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\pi \notin \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, reduced in Spring 1893 to purely elementary polynomial divisibility modulo $p$.
